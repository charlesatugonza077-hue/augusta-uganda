# Augusta Home Laundry: Route and Cost Model

## Decision
Laundry remains a separate Augusta Home add-on, not part of every cleaning membership. Clients should never see a separate boda charge. Transport is built into one clear service or monthly-add-on price.

The lowest-cost, most reliable version is to add laundry to an existing Augusta Home cleaning route: wash, spin-dry and hang on the scheduled service visit; fold and arrange during the next scheduled Home Care visit. A separate fold-only trip should be the exception, not the default.

## Initial coverage area

Start in two service zones rather than accepting every Kampala booking.

### Zone A: Kira-side route
Kyaliwajjala, Najjera, Kira and nearby connected residential areas.

### Zone B: Kampala-side route
Makerere, Kawempe and nearby connected areas.

Do not serve Zone A and Zone B in the same shift during the pilot. Book each zone on a fixed day and cluster at least 3–4 homes per route day. Exact coverage is confirmed from the client’s pin before a quote. Homes outside the active zone get a tailored all-inclusive price or are scheduled for a later route.

## 10-home weekly model

Assumption: 10 clients each take the Weekly Laundry Care add-on. Each client receives four laundry cycles monthly. The fold/arrange work happens during the next planned cleaning visit, not as a separate journey.

| Monthly item | Working assumption | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Revenue | 10 homes × UGX 240,000 | UGX 2,400,000 |
| Laundry worker | Monthly pay range UGX 600,000–700,000 | UGX 600,000–700,000 |
| Route transport | 40 washes ÷ 4 homes per route day = 10 route days × UGX 10,000 | UGX 100,000 |
| Detergent and wash supplies | 40 loads × UGX 4,000–5,000 | UGX 160,000–200,000 |
| Machine recovery and repairs reserve | Depreciation, small repairs and cleaning | UGX 80,000 |
| Scheduling/payment/admin buffer | Mobile-money charges, client messages, small losses | UGX 50,000 |
| Estimated direct operating cost | Range above | UGX 990,000–1,130,000 |
| Contribution before owner pay/tax/major repairs | Revenue minus direct operating costs | UGX 1,270,000–1,410,000 |

That works out to approximately UGX 99,000–113,000 direct cost per weekly client each month, before owner pay/tax. At a UGX 240,000 monthly client price, there is room for transport, supplies and a proper service standard. These figures are a working model and must be replaced with actual pilot figures each week.

## Labour decision

At 10 weekly clients, one dedicated worker at UGX 600,000–700,000 monthly is potentially workable only if: (1) visits are tightly grouped by zone, (2) fold work is paired with scheduled cleaning visits, and (3) the actual laundry workload stays within safe machine capacity.

Do not put the worker on a fixed monthly salary from day one. Start with route-day or per-completed-job pay for the first four-week pilot. Move to a monthly arrangement only after 10 recurring weekly clients have paid for two consecutive months and the route model covers salary, transport, supplies, machine reserve and a business margin.

At UGX 700,000 monthly, labour alone is UGX 70,000 per client per month across 10 clients. At UGX 600,000, it is UGX 60,000. This is why scattered one-off bookings and stand-alone fold trips should not be the main offer.

The client supplies water and electricity because washing happens at their home. Do not make hidden promises around water/electricity; confirm both before booking.

## Route rules

1. One active zone per day.
2. Aim for 3–4 laundry homes in the same area per route day.
3. Use a shared route transport budget of UGX 10,000 per worker/day only where it is realistic; record actual boda spend.
4. If the machine needs a special boda arrangement or cannot safely be carried, use a car/tuk-tuk quote and rebuild the all-inclusive client price before accepting.
5. The service is not confirmed until power, water, drainage and safe hanging space are confirmed.
6. No stand-alone next-day fold visit unless the route is already nearby or the client accepts one all-inclusive tailored price.
7. Review every week: route cost per home, detergent cost per load, time per load, machine issues and repeat bookings.
